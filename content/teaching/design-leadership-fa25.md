## About this Course

This graduate seminar examines foundational and contemporary theories of leadership with a focus on design contexts. We’ll examine the role of leadership in design, and the role design methods and mindsets can play in transforming leadership. Along the way, we’ll be covering the attributes of effective design leaders, historical and evolving models of (design) leadership, and spend time reflecting on leadership challenges that designers face in the 21st century.

### Learning Objectives

1. Traditional and historical theories of management and leadership, and how they relate to the field of design.
2. The key attributes and responsibilities of an effective (design) leader, and strategies to cultivate these qualities.
3. The role that organizational structure and organizational behavior play in creating and nurturing an environment of innovation.
4. The evolving role of leadership in the 21st century, and explore how design is both responding to, and influencing broader organizational and societal shifts.

## Course Mode

This course includes both synchronous and asynchronous components.

The “Getting Started” module also includes an introduction to Zoom@IU, so please make sure to familiarize yourself, update your app etc. before the start of classes. We will meet virtually every Monday from 3pm to 5:30 pm Eastern Time.

This course also uses the IU Canvas learning management system. If you have not previously used Canvas, please log into the course, click on Modules tab on the left, and go through the “Getting Started” Module. The course consists of 4 different activities:

Readings
: The primary readings for this course are chapters from the book Organizational Behavior by Bauer and Erdogan. These would be supplemented by articles, podcasts or papers that provide additional information, or design specific theories. All readings materials will be provided, and I will provide an overview of the readings for the week at the end of every lecture.

Lectures
: The lectures are focused on placing the theories from the readings into a design context, and showing how these can be applied by designers in various stages of leadership, to improve their practice. While the lectures will review concepts from the readings, it is recommended that students complete the readings before class in order to fully understand the lectures.

Essays and Reading Reflections
: Reading reflections (either written essays, or as part of the lecture) are for students to reflect on the readings, as well as how they would apply these theories to their practice, now or in the future. It is a space for students to provide their thoughts and views, question the validity of “conventional” wisdom, reflect on their own experiences, and provide alternative interpretations.

Case Studies
: Case studies are a way for students to think through a potential situation they might encounter, and the various ways they can react to it. They provide a safe space for students to practice the theories learned in the readings and lectures to potential real world scenarios they may face as design leaders, and then reflect on the effectiveness of the theories or tools.

## Grading

### Grading Criteria

Creativity
: The uniqueness of the essays and case study responses.

Thoughtfulness
: How well each of the criteria presented were considered and addressed in the essays, reading reflections, and case study responses.

Rigor
: How well researched, and well considered the final responses were.

Structure
: How well your cases and arguments are structured, and whether you’re able to explain the reasoning and rationale for your decisions in a compelling manner.
