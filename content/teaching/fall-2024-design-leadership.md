## Learning Objectives

1. Understand the difference between management and leadership.
2. Understand traditional and historical theories of management and leadership, and how they relate to the field of design.
3. Understand the attributes of a good (design) leader.
4. Discuss how the role of leadership is changing in the 21st century and how design is reacting to, or driving that change.

## Course Mode

This course includes both synchronous and asynchronous components.

Throughout this term, plan to use Canvas Inbox as your primary way of communicating with me. The course consists of 4 different activities:

Readings
: The primary readings for this course are chapters from the book Organizational Behavior by Bauer and Erdogan. The chapters from this book cover the traditional view of leadership, including Organizational Behavior, Demographic and Diversity, Theories of Motivation, Conflicts and Culture. These would be supplemented by shorter articles, podcasts or papers that provide additional information, or design specific theories.

Lectures
: The lectures are focused on placing the theories from the readings into a design context, and showing how these can be applied by designers in various stages of leadership, to improve their practice. While the lectures will review concepts from the readings, it is recommended that students complete the readings before class in order to fully understand the lectures.

Reading Reflections
: Reading reflections (written, or as part of the lecture) are for students to reflect on the readings, as well as how they would apply these theories to their practice, now or in the future. It is a space for students to provide their thoughts and views, question the validity of “conventional” wisdom, reflect on their own experiences, and provide alternative interpretations.

Case Studies
: Case studies are a way for students to think through a potential situation they might encounter, and the various ways they can react to it. They provide a safe space for students to practice the theories learned in the readings and lectures to potential real world scenarios they may face as design leaders, and then reflect on the effectiveness of the theories or tools.
