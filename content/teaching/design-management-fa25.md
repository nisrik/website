## About this Course

This course covers the practical elements of business and strategic management, and how they relate to design. Through the course, we will be going over the entire business process, through the lens of 4 different contexts within which designers operate. You will learn how to manage people, time, resources, projects, relationships and expectations, and how these elements can come together to build a strong business plan.

### Learning Objectives

1. The different contexts in which designers operate, and how the business and design process differs in each case.
2. The basics of strategic management, as well as the basics of the different functions within an organization, and how they relate to design, or can be used as a design tool.
3. Understand how to build and present a compelling business plan.
4. The skills needed to lead cross-functional teams and navigate organizational challenges using scientific management, operational and evidence based approaches.

## Course Mode

This course includes both synchronous and asynchronous components.

The “Getting Started” module also includes an introduction to Zoom@IU, so please make sure to familiarize yourself, update your app etc. before the start of classes. We will meet virtually every Monday from 3pm to 5:30 pm Eastern Time.

This course also uses the IU Canvas learning management system. If you have not previously used Canvas, please log into the course, click on Modules tab on the left, and go through the “Getting Started” Module. The course consists of 4 different activities:

Readings
: The readings will vary based on the topic for the week, and will be introduced after each lecture. You don’t need to purchase a textbook in this class, all reading material will be provided. I will provide an overview of the readings for the week at the end of every lecture.

Lectures
: The lectures will cover the theoretical elements of the course, and show how they can be used in (a design) practice. The lecture will also include a work-in-progress business case for you to understand how these concepts, frameworks and tools can be used in the context of design.

Case Studies
: Case studies are a way for you to think through a potential situation you might encounter, and the various ways you can react to it. They provide a safe space for you to practice the theories learned in the class and apply them to potential real world scenarios you may face as designers or managers, and then reflect on the effectiveness of the theories or tools.

Business Plan
: Throughout this class, you will have the opportunity to translate what you’ve learned into a full business plan for a business, product or service of your choosing; applying the different concepts and tools, and reflecting on their effectiveness. At the end of the class, you’ll present your business plan in a short pitch presentation, with an accompanying report.

## Grading

### Grading Criteria

Creativity
: The uniqueness of the essays and case study responses.

Thoughtfulness
: How well each of the criteria presented were considered and addressed in the essays, reading reflections, and case study responses.

Rigor
: How well researched, and well considered the final responses were.

Structure
: How well your cases and arguments are structured, and whether you’re able to explain the reasoning and rationale for your decisions in a compelling manner.
