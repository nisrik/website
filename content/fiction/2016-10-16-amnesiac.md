title: Amnesiac

He woke up in a white room.

There was no other way to describe it. White tile walls and floor, and stainless steel equipment. Color? Not in this room. Even the lighting was a fluorescent white.

He looked around at the medical equipment. A clinic? A hospital. A laboratory?

He could feel the cold grey metal of the table against his back. This was no bed he was lying on. He tried to remember what he was doing here. He wondered why he would be here. He then tried to remember anything beyond this moment. Who he was, where he was, how he ended up here. But he drew a blank. Nothing. His mind and memory were as clear and sterile as the room he was in.

His head felt like he had just gone five rounds in the ring with a prizefighter. Not a migraine, but a dull, numbing pain that enveloped his head. As he sat up, he ran his hands over it to find a number of distinct raised scars, and rough stubble. Where the hell was he, and what had they done to him?

At the end of the room, a red light blinked, and a door popped open. From a speaker in the corner came a harsh metallic, but still distinctly female voice: "Ah you're awake. Please exit through the door". He looked for a camera, and sure enough in another corner was a camera. Where the hell was he?

The next room was even sparser. Just a single stainless steel table, and a stainless chair, both bolted to the floor. One door in, and one door out. One wall was covered in what appeared to be a two way mirror. The same red light, speaker and camera populated the corners. He walked up to the mirror to get a closer look at himself. He didn’t know what he looked like to begin with to understand what they had done to him.

"Sit down, please" came the same metallic female voice. He obeyed. Whoever was doing this to him had the answers, and he figured it was better for his sake to co-operate than to be difficult. Besides, what else could he do in this room?

"What is your name?" she asked

"I don't know," he replied.

"What do you want us to call you"

"I don't know"

"Good".

After a brief pause, she said "You are John Smith. You like to be called John or Johnny"

He had no choice but to accept and agree. He couldn't remember being called John. But couldn't remember being called anything else either.

"So, John, tell us about yourself. What do you do?"

"Why are you asking me these things?! I don't know. I can't remember anything!" he pleaded, growing slowly frustrated.

"Good". A single syllable reply, followed by silence.

"Why am I here? What are you doing to me"

"You were sent here John, because you weren't fit for society. You were unlike other people. You stood out. But you weren't dangerous. You were just lost."

"Here we are developing new ways of dealing with people like you. New techniques, so that you wouldn't have to stay locked up forever, and can rejoin society, and live a productive, fulfilling life.

This continued for days. John couldn't remember how many. He couldn't even remember the fact that every night, they would strap a harness onto his head, and pump high voltage current into certain parts of his brain. Unfortunately they underestimated John. And John underestimated the man who put him there.

One night John remembered. He remembered the previous night. The previous day. Vague, memories, but memories none the less.

Day by day he tried to form ideas. Of why he was here. Of what they were doing to him. Of who "they" were. But unfortunately his mind was too far gone. He knew he had to escape. He slowly made a mental map of the facility. Inch by inch, he tried to piece together a plan of this place. He never saw anyone else. He was guided almost exclusively by the voices over the speakers.

"I don't know. I don't remember" he answered, playing along, hoping no one would notice his pupils dilate or his heart rate rise.

Unfortunately he was kept restricted to one area of the facility. He needed to know what was outside.

He waited for someone to slip up. Everyone did eventually. One night his door was left unlocked. He waited for hours, listening carefully to make sure no one would see, no one would notice. When he felt the time was right, he slowly opened the door to his room. He was met with the usual long white corridor with rooms much like his stretching on seemingly endlessly in both directions.

His idea of the facility ended here. He had been transferred once, and retained memories of this corridor. Like the rest of the facility, it was white, sterile, almost inhuman. But his mental floor plan stopped here. He continued down to the right side of the corridor. There was no reason he picked one side over the other. It just felt like he should.

He continued down the corridor, looking for some feature or guide or indicator. Something that wasn’t a white corridor or white door. He didn't look back, which would prove to be a mistake.

He was grabbed from behind, and a cloth stuffed in his face. He tried to struggle, but he was weak.

He woke up in a dark, dimly lit room. He tried to move, but he was strapped into the chair he was placed on. It was starkly different to the white and stainless steel rooms he normally woke up in. But his memories of the room were vague at best, masked by his memories of the initial shock and the voice of the woman.

Leather straps buckled into place held him against a steel chair, which was reclined back. Above him a bright orange light into his face.

As he looked around, he saw he was in an old room. The paint was peeling, everything but the seat he was on bore a heavy layer of dust. There was just one door, and a large glass window on the wall he was facing. Behind the glass he saw a shadowy figure

Have you never wondered why you're here? I'm sure they've given you some bullshit stock answer about how they're going to rehabilitate you. Do you even know how long you've been here?

"No," said John in a meek voice.

"I don't remember much of anything"

Six months.

Ever wondered why they put you here? Asked the shadowy figure, almost rhetorically.

You called yourself the Theologist. You thought of yourself as a preacher, a savior, but in fact were the opposite. You were a terrorist in a way. A terrorist of information. You knew too much about the way the world worked. Too much about what makes everything run the way it does. And you questioned it. You questioned the mechanism and machinations of the world. The way it runs. You tried hard to prove your theories. You devised formulas. You built machines. And you saw something.

I am the reason you are here. I am the reason you don't remember anything. But I didn't understand the importance of what you saw back then. I do now. And I'm going to try and help you remember what it was.

The dark figure flipped a number of switches and turned some knobs. The lights began to flicker as he did so. When he depressed the final switch, a surge of electricity shot into John's head.

His body began to writhe as the powerful jolt travelled through his body into the metal chair. It was a kind of pain he had never experienced before. He tried his level best to escape, but the restraints were strong. "Don't fight it" said the dark figure, his metallic voice ringing across the room. "It will only hurt more. Let it pass into your mind".

John kept trying to fight, but soon realized it was futile. He was at the mercy of this man, and if he wanted to survive, he would have to do as he was told. Besides, this was the first chance he had of regaining his memories.

He calmed down, and tried to slow his breathing. "That's good. Now try to focus the energy in your mind. Try to remember. It might take a while. But try it".

John did as he was told. He focused the energy into his brain. Before long, the shooting pains of the electricity gave way to a warm, numbing feeling that seemed to envelope his brain. The memories came rushing back.

Everything was distant, foggy. It felt like he was looking at someone's life through a frosted pane. Long erased memories of a past life. He saw his graduation. His first house. His first job. A Polaroid from a family vacation. All quick hazy snapshots of the life he must have lived.

"Try and focus on the recent past. Six months ago."

"I don't know how to do that. Tell me what I'm supposed to be looking for"

"Think about a warehouse. A train yard. Maybe a train station." The intensity of the dark figure's voice seemed to have dulled a bit, as for the first time he sensed cooperation from John.

John thought hard, trying to focus the electricity coursing through his mind and body. He managed to sense a pattern of some sort. A linearity to the flow of information. Things from the past appeared darker, more distorted. Memories closer to the present started becoming clearer and clearer.

Behind the glass, the dark figure slowly turned a large, heavy dial on the control board before him. The lights flickered, then dimmed as he did so. This equipment was old, long obsolete. It was all running off a single fuel cell. But it was the only one he could use. The rest of the rooms were tied to a network. Switch one on and everyone would know an illegal access of memories was taking place.

He looked at an old TV screen connected to the device. Grainy, black and white pictures revealed John's memories. As John's brains worked hard to parse the information, the images became clearer.

Images of a train station. An abandoned warehouse. A mess of computer equipment crudely hobbled together. A screen. A blank screen. It hadn't run. Or John was repressing that bit of the memory.

Try harder. You're almost there, said the dark man, his voice taking on an increasingly friendly tone. He continued to rotate the knob as the lights flickered and dimmed. John was sweating, his body convulsing from the force of the electricity coursing through it. And then suddenly it was over. He had been pushed too far and collapsed, the restraints keeping his limp body in place

He woke up in a small alley, behind a large dumpster.

As morning came, he awoke. As he had done the last few months, he tried to remember. Something. Anything. The last thing he could remember was the previous night, when he awoke in this alley, and the rough night he had spent. The cardboard and newspaper had done little to protect him from the cold, and his throat was sore. He couldn’t remember the last time he had eaten. He couldn't remember the last time he had done anything remotely human. As he strained his memory, a name came up. John Smith. Vague memories of an operation came up as well. The large scars on his head reminded him of the same.

He rooted through the pockets of the pants and jacket he had been given. On the cardboard lay the blood soaked hospital scrubs that had woken up in last night, and the garbage bag in which the clothes he was wearing had been packed.

He managed to pull only one thing of any use from the pockets of his jacket:

A single train ticket. It seemed to be for a John Smith. He wondered what his name was. A fragment of a memory came back to him.

"John Smith. Your name is John Smith" said a distant, feminine voice. He struggled to remember anything else. His head was numb from just trying to remember. He touched the scars on his head, wondering what he had gone through. His memories had obviously been wiped. But why? And by whom?.

To what extent could he trust the few things he could remember then?. But then again, what else could he trust? Until someone could prove otherwise, he was John Smith. And this ticket was meant for him.

He turned the ticket over. A crude map had been drawn on the back, in red felt tipped pen. It appeared to show directions from a station, but no clear destination.

Where was he meant to go?

And why?

Who was guiding him? And were they the same people who had wiped his memories and left him here?

He just wished he could remember something. Anything.

He stuffed the ticket back into his pocket. He then wrapped up the blood stained scrubs in the garbage bag, and threw it, along with the newspaper and cardboard into a dumpster.

He looked around the alley. It was tight and densely packed with garbage and fire escapes. There was nothing else there of note.

He walked out towards the street, the ticket in hand. He tried to ask where it was, though the first few times fell on deaf ears. It didn't help that he looked like a street urchin. But he kept at it until someone instructed him it was just a few minutes north.

He walked up to the station. The crowd of people almost overwhelmed him. But something about it, was comforting. Re-assuring in a way. The unkempt station. The writing mass of people.

He boarded the train towards the platform. It was only a couple of stations away, but at this time of the day, the journey was still difficult. The train was filled to the brim. Everyone around him seemed to be in a hurry. Everyone seemed to think they were more important than the rest.

He got off the train. Or rather was pushed off. The crowd was an organism in itself, he was merely a cell. He had to go wherever the crowd wanted to go. Down from the train, and up the steps of the station. At the top he waited for a moment, as the rest of the crowd forced their way around him. His mind started to awaken. The numbness seemed to disappear.

For the first time, it felt familiar. It felt like he had been here before. He HAD been here before. He looked down at the instructions on the ticket.

He knew the answers to the lost half of his life waited just beyond the fence, in an abandoned warehouse. The memories came flooding back.
